## 1. Purpose

To set clear expectations for how staff use company systems and data, so that
acceptable behavior is obvious before anyone has to guess.

## 2. Scope

All employees and contractors, and all company devices, accounts, networks,
and data — including personal devices used to access company systems.

## 3. Acceptable use

Staff may use company systems for company business, and for incidental
personal use that does not interfere with work, consume significant
resources, or violate this policy.

## 4. Unacceptable use

The following are prohibited on company systems, without exception:

1. Accessing, copying, or sharing customer data outside the scope of your
   role, or with any person or system not authorized to receive it.
2. Sharing credentials, bypassing MFA or access controls, or using another
   person's account.
3. Installing unapproved software, browser extensions, or AI tools that
   transmit company or customer data to a third party.
4. Storing company or customer data on personal cloud storage, personal
   email, or unencrypted removable media.
5. Disabling or circumventing security tooling (endpoint protection, disk
   encryption, VPN, logging).
6. Using company systems for illegal activity, harassment, or to access,
   store, or distribute unlawful material.
7. Connecting unknown USB devices or untrusted networks to systems holding
   customer data without approval.
8. Training public AI models on, or pasting into public AI tools, any
   customer data, credentials, or non-public company information.

## 5. Devices and AI tools

1. Company devices must have disk encryption, endpoint protection, and
   automatic updates enabled. Lost or stolen devices are reported to
   [security contact] immediately.
2. Personal devices used for company work must meet the same baseline
   (encryption, screen lock, current OS) and enroll in [MDM / access policy]
   where required.
3. AI tools are approved per-tool by [policy owner]. Approved tools and
   their permitted data classes are listed in [approved-tools register].
   Unlisted tools are not approved by default.

## 6. Monitoring

Staff should have no expectation of privacy on company systems. [Company
Name] may monitor systems, networks, and accounts for security and
compliance purposes, within applicable law. Monitoring is used to protect
the company and its customers, not to surveil individuals.

## 7. Reporting

Suspected violations or security events are reported to [security contact]
immediately. Good-faith reporting is protected: nobody is penalized for
raising a genuine concern.

## 8. Enforcement

Violations are addressed through [Company Name]'s disciplinary process, up
to and including termination. Violations involving customer data are
escalated to the policy owner the same day.

## 12. Where startups fail this control

1. **Signatures with no training behind them.** An acknowledgment proves
   someone signed, not that they understood. Auditors pair the signature
   with the training record — one without the other is thin.
2. **The AI rule exists but shadow AI use goes undetected.** Banning
   customer data in public AI tools is easy to write and hard to enforce.
   Auditors ask how you detect it, not just whether you prohibit it — the
   approved-tools register plus endpoint/egress visibility is the answer.
3. **No approved-tools list at all.** A rule that says "approved tools
   only" with no list of which tools are approved is unenforceable, and
   auditors treat it as unwritten.
