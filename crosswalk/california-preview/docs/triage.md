# Startup triage — which California AI laws apply to a typical startup

> **NOT YET ATTORNEY-REVIEWED.** This preview has not been reviewed by a
> California-licensed attorney. It is an educational starting point, not legal
> advice and not a substitute for advice tailored to your company.

## Method

**"Typical startup"** means a small US B2B SaaS/AI company, seed to Series B,
roughly 10–100 people, selling to enterprises, with no SOC 2 attestation yet.
It builds software (often AI features or a GenAI product), hires in California
or screens California applicants, and processes customer and employee data. It
does not train frontier-scale models and does not have $500M in revenue.

**Sources.** Thresholds, triggers, and dates come from the official text as
verified in the crosswalk's source-verification pass (chaptered bill texts and
the OAL-approved regulation texts, read 21 Nov 2026) and encoded in
`data/ca_requirements.csv` (39 requirements) and `data/ca_mappings.csv`
(123 mappings). Where the official text could not be verified it is marked
**UNVERIFIED** and listed in "UNVERIFIED items" below. This is not legal
advice, and no mapping here guarantees compliance.

**How to read the verdicts.** *Applies* = a typical startup in the stated
situation is covered. *Probably not* = covered only in an edge case. *Only
if* = covered only when the named trigger is met. ISO 42001 controls listed
are the ones with a Partial mapping to that law — they help with the duty,
they do not satisfy it on their own (there are no Full mappings anywhere in
the module).

**Cross-law note.** No federal statute preempts any California AI law as of
21 November 2026. An executive order alone does not preempt state law.
Executive Order 14365, signed 11 December 2025, directs federal agencies to
challenge state AI laws and asks Congress to pass a preemptive federal law,
but it repeals nothing by itself, names only Colorado, and Congress has
passed no such law. The Department of Justice created an AI Litigation Task
Force on 9 January 2026 to challenge state AI laws; it was folded into the
Super Intelligence Task Force announced 5 November 2026, which has announced
no new case against any state AI law. The Commerce Department evaluation of
"onerous" state AI laws, due 11 March 2026, has no published copy in the
public record. DOJ's only court action is its 24 April 2026 intervention in
*X.AI v. Weiser* (D. Colo. No. 1:26-cv-01515-DDD-CYC), challenging Colorado
SB 24-205; enforcement was suspended 27 April 2026 and Colorado replaced the
law with SB 26-189 in May 2026. No California law has been named or targeted
in any federal document we could verify. Preemption is a watch item, not a
current qualification on any duty below.

## Per-law notes that do not fit the table

- **SB 53 — whistleblower protections (Lab. Code §1107.1).** Covered employees (those responsible for assessing, managing or addressing critical-safety-incident risk) are protected when they disclose catastrophic-risk information or an SB 53 violation. The developer must give notice of these rights by continuous workplace posting (with equivalent notice to new and remote employees) or annual written notice with receipt acknowledged. Large developers must also run a reasonable anonymous internal process, give monthly status updates, and share disclosures quarterly with officers and directors. Contractors are not covered employees. **UNVERIFIED:** the official text of Lab. Code §1107.2 (U-01) and §§1107–1107.1 (U-02) has still not been read directly — but the content is substantively settled: §1107.2 is a one-sentence interpretive rule with no developer duty (Justia full text, 21 Nov 2026), and the §§1107–1107.1 text matches the module with no discrepancy found (Justia full text + AG page, 21 Nov 2026).
- **AB 2013 — the 12-item minimum.** §3111 requires a high-level training-data summary covering *at least* 12 items (dataset sources/owners, purpose, data-point counts in general ranges, data types, IP-protected vs public domain, purchased/licensed, personal information, cleaning/processing, collection period, first-use dates, synthetic data). The section is a minimum, not a checklist — a summary that covers only the 12 items may still be incomplete. **UNVERIFIED:** the codified §§3110–3111 text on leginfo (verified via Justia only, U-04); the Ninth Circuit decision in No. 26-1591 is pending (U-05). The PI-denial order date is confirmed as 4 Mar 2026 — the order itself was read in Fix Pass 2 (Dkt. 35); the 5 Mar date in one secondary source is a reporting error.
- **CCPA ADMT — three secondary-only details.** The 12-month re-ask rule (§7221(k)), the aggregate-response rule (§7222(j)), and the access timing (§7021) are now VERIFIED against the official text (Fix Pass 2, 21 Nov 2026). The §7157/§7124 submission portals have not been confirmed live (U-06) — check the CPPA website before the 1 Apr 2028 deadlines. The CPPA fine figures ($2,663 / $7,988 per violation) are secondary-sourced only and are not cited in this triage.
- **SB 942 — the AG report mechanism.** §22757.3(c) requires the Attorney General to establish a mechanism to receive provider reports of licensee noncompliance, but whether that mechanism exists is **UNVERIFIED** (U-08) — no mechanism was found in Fix Pass 2 searches of oag.ca.gov (21 Nov 2026), so a provider's (b)(4) report still has no confirmed destination. The codified §§22757.1–22757.6 text was not directly read (leginfo returned HTTP 403; verified against the SB 1000/AB 2713/AB 853 chaptered texts, U-07).
- **FEHA — what the module does not yet cover.** Five requirements have no ISO control mapped (see the table). The conforming ADS insertions in §§11028–11079 were not individually re-read in Pass 1 — one omission found in Fix Pass 2: §11038 also carries the §11009(f) formula (see CA-FEHA-11009 Notes). The OAL register check is now VERIFIED: the entry is in Register 2025, No. 28-Z (11 Jul 2025), pp. 886–887 — not No. 27-Z as an earlier draft directed; filed 27 Jun 2025, effective 1 Oct 2025. The §11072 parent-subdivision citation is re-confirmed (§11072(b)(5), item (F)). Related litigation applies the regulations rather than challenging them: *Mobley v. Workday* (N.D. Cal.) and *Does 1–26 v. Meta* (N.D. Cal. No. 3:26-cv-07122, filed Jul 2026).
- **SB 947 — two attorney flags.** §1526.1(c) says "deactivation" where the rest of the part says "termination" — do not paraphrase it away. "Primarily relies" is undefined and no Labor Commissioner guidance exists yet (U-10; re-checked 21 Nov 2026, none found). The codified §§1520–1526.7 display was not directly read (U-09).
- **SB 813 / AB 1405 — the GovOps report.** The report "AI Safety in 2026 and Beyond" (dated 16 Nov, released 18 Nov 2026) recommending mandatory IVO oversight was verified against the report PDF itself in Fix Pass 2 (govops.ca.gov, 21 Nov 2026) — still a proposal, not law. The codified Gov. Code text was not directly read (U-11).

## Verdict summary

- **AB 2013:** Applies — if you built a GenAI system Californians can use, you owe the training-data summary.
- **SB 942:** Applies if you create, code, or otherwise produce a GenAI system publicly accessible in California.
- **CCPA ADMT:** Only if you are a CCPA-covered business and do one of the six trigger activities.
- **FEHA ADS:** Applies — if you employ 5+ people and use an ADS in hiring or employment in California.
- **SB 947:** Only if you use an ADS for discipline or termination decisions — and not before 1 Jul 2027.
- **SB 53:** Probably not — only if you trained a frontier model (>10^26 operations).
- **SB 813 / AB 1405:** Probably not — only if you sell AI audit services or choose to engage a designated verifier.
